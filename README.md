# An Analytical and Computational Investigation of the Riemann Hypothesis

## Overview

This project aims to investigate the computational and numerical properties of the Riemann zeta function through the development and analysis of high-precision numerical algorithms, zero-detection methods, and validated computational techniques.

The primary objective is to explore the behavior of the Riemann zeta function along the critical strip, with particular emphasis on the critical line

$$s=\frac{1}{2}+it$$

and to investigate computational methods for approximating and analyzing its nontrivial zeros.

The project also explores connections between the Riemann Hypothesis and arithmetic number theory, including the Lagarias criterion, numerical stability, and the accuracy of computational methods.

The implementation will primarily use **C++** for computational algorithms and **MATLAB** for numerical analysis, validation, and visualization.

---

## Mathematical Background

### 1. The Riemann Zeta Function

For complex numbers \(s\) with \(\operatorname{Re}(s)>1\), the Riemann zeta function is defined by

$$
\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^s}.
$$

Through analytic continuation, the zeta function extends to the complex plane, except for a simple pole at \(s=1\).

The Riemann Hypothesis states that every nontrivial zero of the zeta function has real part equal to

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Re}(s)=\frac{1}{2}}.
$$

The project will investigate numerical evaluations of

$$
\zeta\left(\frac12+it\right)
$$

over a specified range of imaginary components.

### 2. Critical-Line Zero Detection

To investigate zeros along the critical line, the project will consider the Hardy \(Z\)-function:

$$
Z(t)=e^{i\theta(t)}
\zeta\left(\frac12+it\right),
$$

where

$$
\theta(t)=
\operatorname{Im}\log\Gamma\left(\frac14+\frac{it}{2}\right)
-\frac{t}{2}\log\pi.
$$

For real \(t\), the Hardy \(Z\)-function is real-valued.

Consequently, locating real zeros of \(Z(t)\) provides a method for identifying zeros of the zeta function on the critical line.

The investigation will consider numerical root-finding, sign-change detection, root isolation, and comparisons with known zero ordinates.

### 3. Lagarias Criterion

The project will also investigate the Lagarias criterion, which provides an equivalent formulation of the Riemann Hypothesis.

Let

$$
\sigma(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}d
$$

denote the sum-of-divisors function, and let

$$
H_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac1k
$$

denote the \(n\)-th harmonic number.

The Lagarias criterion states that the Riemann Hypothesis is true if and only if

$$
\boxed{
\sigma(n)\leq H_n+e^{H_n}\log H_n
}
$$

for every positive integer \(n\).

Define the residual

$$
L(n)=H_n+e^{H_n}\log H_n-\sigma(n).
$$

The computational investigation will examine the behavior of this residual over finite integer ranges.

A finite computational verification does not prove the criterion for all positive integers and therefore cannot establish the Riemann Hypothesis.

## Numerical Analysis

A major component of the project will be evaluating the reliability of the computed results.

For a numerical approximation \(\widehat{f}(x)\) and a reference value \(f_{\mathrm{ref}}(x)\), the absolute error is defined as

$$
E_{\mathrm{abs}}=
|\widehat{f}(x)-f_{\mathrm{ref}}(x)|.
$$

When the reference value is sufficiently far from zero, relative error can be calculated as

$$
E_{\mathrm{rel}}=
\frac{|\widehat{f}(x)-f_{\mathrm{ref}}(x)|}
{|f_{\mathrm{ref}}(x)|}.
$$

The investigation will examine:

- Floating-point rounding error.
- Accumulation error.
- Cancellation and loss of significance.
- Precision-dependent discrepancies.
- Algorithmic convergence.
- Error propagation through mathematical expressions.

Particular attention will be given to residuals close to zero, where ordinary floating-point comparisons may be unreliable.

Where feasible, interval arithmetic and explicit numerical error bounds will be used to establish validated results.

## Validation and Limitations

Numerical evidence must be distinguished from mathematical proof.

In particular:

- Evaluating the zeta function over a finite range does not establish that all nontrivial zeros lie on the critical line.
- Detecting sign changes in the Hardy \(Z\)-function does not guarantee that every zero has been found.
- Checking the Lagarias inequality for finitely many integers does not establish that it holds for all positive integers.
- Agreement between two numerical implementations does not automatically guarantee correctness if they share the same assumptions or errors.

The project will therefore prioritize independent verification, numerical error analysis, and reproducibility.

## Research Scope

The objective is not to claim a proof or disproof of the Riemann Hypothesis through finite computation.

The emphasis is on numerical accuracy, algorithmic reliability, computational efficiency, and the mathematical interpretation of the resulting data.
